The Land

6.8 Hectares of
Living Soil

Three cultivation zones, two natural dams, and a closed loop that returns everything to the earth. This is how Hudson's Farm works.

Cultivation Zones

What grows where

Hudson's Farm is divided into three active growing zones, each with a specific role in the farm's regenerative system. No zone works in isolation — they all feed one another.

1
Zone 1 · Top · 1 hectare

Hemp Cultivation

The top zone is dedicated to licensed, certified organic hemp. Grown using no-till methods on living soil, the hemp is managed rotationally to prevent pest build-up and allow the soil to rest between cycles.

Industrial hemp No-till Rotational Licensed
2
Zone 2 · Centre · 2.5 hectares

Food Garden & Microgreens

The central zone is the farm's most active production area. Seasonal vegetables, culinary herbs, and edible flowers grow alongside the microgreens nursery — all harvested fresh and supplied directly to restaurants, households, and weekly subscribers.

Seasonal vegetables Herbs Microgreens Direct supply
3
Zone 3 · South · 3.3 hectares

Hemp & Organic Production

The largest zone combines hemp cultivation with expanded organic food production. This zone supplies the bulk of the farm's hemp harvest and provides additional growing space for the food garden's seasonal overflow — including the pecan orchard that lines the southern boundary.

Hemp Organic food Pecan orchard Seasonal

Regenerative System

The closed loop

Every element of Hudson's Farm feeds another. Nothing leaves the cycle — it all returns to the soil. This is what "regenerative" actually means in practice.

Natural Dams

Two permanent dams fed by rain and underground springs supply all irrigation

Borehole Irrigation

Water drawn from the borehole drip-irrigates all three cultivation zones

Living Crops

Hemp, mushrooms, microgreens, and vegetables grow in rotating chemical-free cycles

Compost Cycle

Crop residues, hemp stalks, and organic matter are composted back into the soil

Living Soil

Nutrients return to the earth — leaving the soil richer than the previous season
